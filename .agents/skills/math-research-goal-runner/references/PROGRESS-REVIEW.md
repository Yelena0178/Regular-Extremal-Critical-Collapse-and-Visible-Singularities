# Strategic plateau review

Use this protocol during active research and on recovery. It detects sustained fixation on a core problem even when useful local work continues. It does not create a scheduler or change runtime goal status.

## Observe the frontier, not output volume

A research round is one concrete question/attempt, its bounded audit, and a saved result or failure plus next action. Tool calls, document edits and chat turns are not rounds. Check health at each round end and roughly every 30 active minutes during a long attempt, at the next safe tool boundary.

Record two separate signals:

- **Local progress:** a valid identity, lemma, implementation fix, improved estimate, computation or source audit.
- **Strategic movement:** an inspected result closes or materially reduces a necessary gap, establishes a previously missing usable input, or rules out a live mechanism/candidate sufficiently to change the next research decision. Name the acceptance gate, old obstacle, new evidence and changed consequence. Whole-goal closure is not required.

An auxiliary result counts strategically only with that concrete bridge. Renaming a norm, improving irrelevant precision, adding conditional interfaces with the same unverified premise, writing a review, changing route names, or merely proposing a new method does not reset the strategic clock. A new failure of an already excluded mechanism adds no strategic movement. Keep later falsification of a claimed advance visible; downgrade affected claims and restore the last valid baseline where the record permits.

## Persist minimal research-health state

Map these fields into STATE/CHECKPOINT, outside the installed skill:

- Bottleneck signature: necessary obligation, missing input and mechanism; retain identity across renamed claims or notation.
- Last valid strategic advance: round, dated evidence/claim IDs, before/after gap and audit scope.
- Rounds and cumulative active research minutes since that advance; local advances listed separately.
- Active route and equivalent failure signatures; last review, its decision and the next discriminating test.
- Review thresholds, next review deadline and previous sync receipt/pending work.

Active minutes include actual research and running computations; exclude offline time and explicit user pauses. At startup establish or recover a baseline. Use known records for historical rounds; mark missing timing unknown rather than inventing durations. Preserve counters across chats, route changes and reviews. A reviewed plan alone never resets the strategic baseline.

## Review triggers

Defaults are operational settings, adjustable to the project with a recorded reason; they are not evidence standards:

| Trigger | Action |
|---|---|
| Same core gap persists for 5 research rounds OR 180 active minutes without strategic movement | Strategic review, even with new local results every round |
| Two materially equivalent failed attempts | Review the mechanism before repeating |
| New evidence contradicts a core premise, or the route requires an input as hard as the original problem | Immediate review of the route and affected dependencies |

Check time during long rounds, rather than postponing the review indefinitely until an attempt ends. A long computation is assessed for health, expected information and cost; do not automatically kill a valuable job. If a review has just occurred, do not repeat the same review on every poll: absent strategic movement, review again after 2 further rounds or 60 further active minutes, whichever comes first. New fatal evidence can trigger earlier review. Budget exhaustion remains a runtime boundary, not a mathematical failure.

## Required review and action

1. Compare the current critical graph to the last strategic baseline: what has actually changed, and which exact missing input remains unchanged? Identify useful local work without presenting it as a breakthrough.
2. Diagnose the shared obstruction: implementation, insufficient estimate, unjustified premise, wrong representation, unsuitable candidate/mechanism, or evidence against the proposed target. Look for circular dependencies and repeated transfer of the same difficulty into a new interface.
3. Examine genuinely different routes where plausible: a new mechanism/candidate, a different mathematical representation with justified conversion, counterexample/no-go analysis, a different theorem interface, or a small discriminating experiment. Do not manufacture alternatives to meet a quota.
4. Select a route and the smallest test that could distinguish it from the stalled route. State expected observations, rejection/abandonment criterion, effort budget and review deadline. Then execute the test in the available run; do not end with a generic recommendation to change approach.
5. Default to a change addressing the diagnosed obstruction. A bounded continuation of the old route needs concrete new evidence or a demonstrably unfinished discriminating step, an explicit payoff and a deadline. Do not repeatedly renew the same justification without new evidence.
6. After two reviews without strategic movement, escalate from local technique to candidate/mechanism and problem formulation. Revisit applicability of assumptions, known obstructions and whether the target may be false. Any change to the goal contract still needs user authorization; route changes do not. If no justified next step is available, report the precise missing resource or decision and preserve open status under runtime rules.

Persist a compact review receipt: trigger, baseline/current gap, local gains, diagnosis, alternatives considered, selected change, executable test, budget/deadline and affected claims. Link it from the checkpoint and include it in the authorized round sync. Use the user's language. Report strategic progress by evidence and changed obligations, never by a claimed breakthrough percentage.
